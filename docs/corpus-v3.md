# The v3 corpus: when held-out accuracy stopped predicting strength

> **About the numbers in this document.** Every measurement here comes from
> a specific run of a specific checkpoint, and the checkpoints live under
> `runs/`, which is gitignored — so nothing here can be verified from a
> fresh clone alone. Two things are worth checking before trusting a figure:
> **which learning rate it was measured at**, and **when**. Anything dated
> before 2026-08-30 was measured at `--lr 2e-3`, a rate chosen for the
> ResNet and inherited by every architecture added later; two of the four
> were being trained at the wrong one, and correcting that reversed several
> conclusions rather than merely shifting decimals. See
> `learning-rate-sweep.md`. Regenerate everything with
> `scripts/evaluate_lineup.sh`.

The oracle benchmark visited 17,062 positions at plies 3-6 that the corpus
did not contain. Solving them exactly and folding the labels in produced
`exact-sampled-v3.npz`, and retraining `cpool` on it produced the most
useful negative result in this project so far.

## The experiment

One variable. `v3-cpool` uses the same architecture, the same swept learning
rate (6e-4), the same seed (20260828) and the same sixteen epochs as the
published `swept-cpool`. Only the corpus differs.

From scratch rather than warm-started, deliberately. `retrain-and-finetune.md`
argues that a warm start is the better *practice* when a corpus grows, and
that is true — but it is the wrong *experiment*, because it conflates a
better corpus with more total training.

| | v2 | v3 |
|---|---|---|
| rows | 3,196,958 | 3,520,526 |
| policy-labelled | 255,058 | 271,676 |
| ply 5 | 22,655 | 29,905 |
| ply 6 | 86,631 | 170,766 |

The 17,062 solved positions became 380,041 rows — about 22 free child value
labels each. **Verified directly: v3 shares zero canonical keys with the
7,800-position probe**, which is the invariant every number below rests on.

### Where the labels come from

Worth stating precisely, because "trained on its own games" would invalidate
everything here and it is not what happens.

Autoplay contributes **only the choice of which positions to solve**. No game
result is ever a label. The exact solver returns, per position, `won`,
`outcome_optimal` (every move preserving the win) and `action_values` (an
exact score for every legal action). Parent rows take `outcome_optimal` as
the policy target and ±1 from `won`; child rows are generated by applying
each legal action and negating the parent's exact `action_value`
(`scripts/build_oracle_corpus.py`).

Two consequences. **The value target is a sign, not a margin** — the solver's
exact scores are collapsed to ±1, so the value head is a win/loss classifier
rather than an evaluation function. And **the self-selection is about
distribution, not labels**: the games decide which positions get exact
labels, so the corpus tilts toward the matchups played.

## The result

**On the held-out probe, the two models are the same.**

| band | v2 → v3 | McNemar |
|---|---|---|
| all (plies 4-12) | +0.0002 | p = 1.0 |
| shallow 4-6 | +0.0062 | p = 0.31 |
| deep 7-12 | **−0.0047** | **p = 0.033** |
| ply 4 | **+0.0359** | **p = 0.006** |
| ply 5 | −0.0161 | p = 0.13 |

**In play, they are not.**

| condition | v2's win rate vs v3 | |
|---|---|---|
| policy, ply 3 | 42.8% [39.8, 45.9] | v3 wins |
| policy, ply 6 | 49.1% [46.0, 52.2] | tie |
| MCTS-128, ply 3 | 45.1% [41.7, 48.6] | v3 wins |
| MCTS-128, ply 6 | 48.9% [45.4, 52.3] | tie |

And `v3-cpool` **beats `minimax-d2` at 59.7%** [57.5, 61.8] — the first model
here to do so. `swept-cpool` scored 48.9% on the same seed, reproducing its
independent 49.4% from three other seeds.

**So the held-out policy accuracy this project ranks architectures with did
not predict play strength.** That is the finding. It is the second signal
pointing the same way: `attn` finishes last under search despite ranking
second on raw policy (`oracle-benchmark.md`).

What moved was the value head — MAE **0.0777 → 0.0351**, sign accuracy
0.9646 → 0.9859.

## The confound, and how it was tested

v3's added positions came from network-versus-`minimax-d2` games, so the
oracle result is suspect on its own: the model is measured on the matchup its
training distribution was drawn from.

Net-versus-net positions are **not** in v3's added data — those came from the
earlier swept arena, which was never solved or merged. So the field arena is
the clean test, and v3 passes it at ply 3: it beats `attn` 66.7%, `resnet`
64.2% and `mlp` 66.3%, all significant.

## What the advantage actually is

Not "better". **Depth-dependent.**

| | policy p3 | policy p6 | MCTS p3 | MCTS p6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cpool-v3` | **63.1%** | 51.8% | **67.4%** | 55.8% (4th) |
| `cpool` (v2) | 52.7% | 53.0% | 64.4% | **57.7%** |
| `attn` | 48.3% | **53.6%** | 53.9% | 57.0% |
| `resnet` | 46.5% | 46.8% | 58.7% | 56.5% |
| `mlp` | 39.5% | 44.7% | 55.0% | 53.5% |

Six measurements, one seed, all consistent: dominant from ply-3 starts, at or
below v2 from ply 6. That matches where the corpus grew — a ply-3 start
traverses the newly-labelled plies 5-6; a ply-6 start begins at its edge.

At ply 6 the four leading intervals overlap heavily, so **no claim should
rest on the ply-6 ordering.**

## A prediction that failed

The value MAE halved, and search leans on the leaf value, so search should
have amplified v3's advantage. It does the opposite: the policy gap at ply 3
is 14.4 points and the 128-simulation gap is 9.8.

The reasoning was asymmetric and should not have been trusted. A value head
bad enough to mislead a search costs rank — which is what `attn` shows.
Improving an already-good one mostly duplicates what the simulations were
resolving anyway.

## What is being tested next, and why not seeds

The deep-band regression is 0.0047 on a band sitting at 0.987. Before
reaching for the architecture, the cheap causes have to be excluded, and the
training curves point at the cheapest one.

**Overfitting is not the constraint.** Train-minus-validation top-1 at the
final epoch is under one point for every model in the family — `mlp` −0.0005,
`resnet` +0.0021, `swept-cpool` +0.0067, `swept-attn` +0.0082, `v3-cpool`
+0.0100 — and peak-to-final decay is at most 0.0012. The models plateau; they
do not degrade. So more epochs is low-risk, and **underfitting** is the
likelier explanation: a 10% larger corpus given the same sixteen epochs is
~10% fewer gradient steps per position.

The order of things to try, cheapest first: the epoch budget (`--patience`,
which exists for exactly this); then the loss balance, since policy loss is
normalised by the sum of policy weights while value loss averages over
*every* row and the value-only fraction is now 92.28%; then capacity; and
only then structure.

**Seeds come after the protocol, not before.** This project already made that
call once, during the learning-rate flaw — a second seed was queued and
cancelled, because replicating a possibly-unfair configuration is the wrong
thing to spend compute on. The fixed epoch budget is the same class of flaw.

## Nothing from v3 is published

The Hub repos carry the v2 weights. Publishing a checkpoint that is better
only from shallow starts, on the strength of one seed, with the ply-6
orderings inside each other's intervals, is not a defensible thing to hand a
downloader.
